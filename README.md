Abstract

Environmental heterogeneity (EH) is a fundamental driver of biodiversity, yet empirical heterogeneity–diversity relationships (HDRs) vary widely in form, limiting their generality across taxa and spatial scales. The area–heterogeneity trade-off (AHTO) hypothesis proposes that increasing EH creates additional niche opportunities while reducing the effective area available to individual species, but this mechanism has rarely been tested across broad environmental gradients and taxonomic groups.

Here we develop an integrated framework linking species-energy, niche, and island biogeography theories and evaluate its predictions across 868 tree, 584 bird, and 122 mammal species throughout the contiguous United States. We quantify EH from the multidimensional dissimilarity of AlphaEarth Foundation Model embeddings, integrating climatic, topographic, edaphic, structural, and compositional variation within a common representation.

Across taxa, our results support the AHTO framework, with the clearest patterns in trees and birds: species richness shows a unimodal relationship with EH, whereas mean species abundance shows a previously undocumented U-shaped relationship. The richness maximum and abundance minimum shift toward higher EH with increasing spatial scale. Functional traits further predict species response, with acquisitive, fast-living, broad-niched, well-dispersing species responding more positively to EH.

These results establish AHTO as a general organizing principle whose expression depends on spatial scale and species functional traits, providing a unified basis for understanding when EH promotes or constrains biodiversity.

Data Sources

Data associated with this project came from the following datasets.

1. Trees
Distribution and abundance: USDA Forest Inventory and Analysis (FIA) database
Traits: TRY (Kattge et al., 2011) and Botanical Information and Ecology Network (BIEN) (Maitner et al., 2018)
Phylogeny: GBOTB.extended.TPL (Jin & Qian, 2022)
2. Birds
Distribution and abundance: North American Breeding Bird Survey (BBS)
Traits: AVONET (Tobias et al., 2022) and BIRDBASE (Şekercioğlu et al., 2025)
Phylogeny: A complete, time-scaled, evolutionary tree of the world's bird species (McTavish et al., 2025)
Range polygons: BirdLife International
3. Mammals
Distribution and abundance: SNAPSHOT USA 2019–2023 (Rooney et al., 2025)
Traits: PanTHERIA (Jones et al., 2009) and EltonTraits 1.0 (Wilman et al., 2014)
Phylogeny: VertLife (Upham et al., 2019)
Range polygons: IUCN Red List
4. Environmental Heterogeneity
AlphaEarth Foundations (AEF) embeddings (Brown et al., 2025)
5. Environmental Covariates
Climate: WorldClim 2
Soil properties: SoilGrids 2.0 (Poggio et al., 2021) and Soil Moisture Active Passive (SMAP) soil moisture (O'Neill et al., 2021)
Elevation: Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM) digital elevation model
Net primary production: MODIS Terra MOD17A3 product
