# Re-evalutate_Heterogeneity_Diversity_Relationship_across_Taxa_and_Scale
This project contains code related to the paper titled: Re-evaluating the Environmental Heterogeneity–Diversity Relationship Across Taxa and Spatial Scales 
# Paper Abstract
Environmental heterogeneity (EH) is a fundamental driver of biodiversity, yet empirical heterogeneity–diversity relationships (HDRs) vary widely in form, limiting their generality across taxa and spatial scales. The area–heterogeneity trade-off (AHTO) hypothesis proposes that increasing EH creates additional niche opportunities while reducing the effective area available to individual species, but this mechanism has rarely been tested across broad environmental gradients and taxonomic groups. Here we develop an integrated framework linking species-energy, niche, and island biogeography theories and evaluate its prediction across 868 tree, 584 bird, and 122 mammal species throughout the contiguous United States. We quantify EH from the multidimensional dissimilarity of AlphaEarth Foundation Model embeddings, integrating climatic, topographic, edaphic, structural, and compositional variation within a common representation. Across taxa, our results support the AHTO framework, with the clearest patterns in trees and birds: species richness shows a unimodal relationship with EH, whereas mean species abundance shows a previously undocumented U-shaped relationship. The richness maximum and abundance minimum shift toward higher EH with increasing spatial scale. Functional traits further predict species response, with acquisitive, fast-living, broad-niched, well-dispersing species respond more positively to EH. These results establish AHTO as a general organizing principle whose expression depends on spatial scale and species functional traits, providing a unified basis for understanding when EH promotes or constrains biodiversity.

# Data
Data associated with this project came from the following datasets:
 1. Tree data:
    Distribution and abundance data:  USDA Forest Inventory and Analysis (FIA) database
    Trait data: TRY (Kattge et al., 2011) and Botanical Information and Ecology Network (BIEN) (Maitner et al., 2018)
    Phylogeny data: GBOTB.extended.TPL (Jin & Qian, 2022)
 2. Bird data:
    Distribution and abundance data: North American Breeding Bird Survey (BBS)
    Trait data: AVONET (Tobias et al., 2022) and BIRDBASE (Şekercioğlu et al., 2025)
    Phylogeny data: A complete, time-scaled, evolutionary tree of the world’s bird species (McTavish et al., 2025)
    Range polygons: BirdLife International 
 3. Mammal data:
    Distribution and abundance data: SNAPSHOT USA 2019–2023 (Rooney et al., 2025)
    Trait data: PanTHERIA (Jones et al., 2009) and EltonTraits 1.0 (Wilman et al., 2014)
    Phylogeny data: VertLife (Upham et al., 2019)
    Range polygons: IUCN Red List
 4. Environmental heterogeneity: AlphaEarth Foundations (AEF) embeddings (Brown et al., 2025)
 5. Environmental covariates:
    Climate data: WorldClim2
    Soil properties: SoilGrids 2.0 (Poggio, 2021) and Soil moisture from Soil Moisture Active Passive (SMAP) (ONeill et al., 2021)
    Elevation: Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM) digital elevation model
    Net primary production: MODIS Terra MOD17A3 product
